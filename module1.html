Module 1: Core Understanding 鈥?Autism & Dyslexia

Build a foundation of knowledge to create a more effective and inclusive classroom.

[Embedded Video Player: "Seeing the Brilliance in Every Mind" - 2 min]

Key Takeaways:

  • Dyslexia is a common learning difference in reading/spelling, not a sign of low intelligence.
  • Autism is a neurodevelopmental condition that affects communication, social interaction, and sensory processing.
  • Students with both profiles have unique strengths that can be leveraged in the classroom.
  • Your role is to adapt your teaching to their needs, not to "fix" the student.
馃挕 UDL Connection: Understanding learner variability is the first principle of Universal Design for Learning. This module helps you recognize and value the diverse ways your students think and learn.
馃摎 Understanding Dyslexia: A Learning Difference UDL

Simple Definition: A common learning difference that primarily affects reading and spelling. It is unrelated to intelligence. It's about how the brain processes language.

What You Might Observe in Class:

  • Reading: Slow, effortful reading; guessing words based on context; difficulty sounding out unfamiliar words
  • Spelling & Writing: Inconsistent spelling (e.g., "wuz" for "was"); messy handwriting; difficulty organizing thoughts in writing
  • Memory & Sequencing: Trouble remembering sequences like days of the week or multi-step instructions

Strength-Based Perspective:

Students with dyslexia often develop remarkable strengths to compensate:

Problem-Solving
Creative thinking and innovation
Big Picture Thinking
Seeing connections others miss
Visual-Spatial Skills
Strong in arts, engineering, design
Empathy & Narrative
Understanding stories and emotions
In a Kazakhstani Classroom: Challenges with reading and sound-processing may appear in both Kazakh and Russian languages. The core strategies you'll learn work across languages.

馃 Understanding Autism: A Different Way of Thinking UDL

Simple Definition: A neurodevelopmental condition that affects how a person communicates, interacts, and experiences the world. It is a spectrum, meaning every autistic pupil is unique.

What You Might Observe in Class:

  • Social Interaction: Difficulty understanding unspoken social rules; may prefer parallel play or working alone
  • Communication: May use language literally (confused by idioms like "break a leg"); may have intense knowledge on specific topics
  • Sensory Sensitivities: May be overwhelmed by loud noises, bright lights, or certain textures; may seek sensory input
  • Routines & Predictability: Strong preference for familiar routines; distress with unexpected changes

Strength-Based Perspective:

Autistic thinkers bring incredible strengths to the classroom:

Intense Focus
Deep concentration on interests
Attention to Detail
Noticing patterns others miss
Honesty & Fairness
Strong sense of justice
Creative Thinking
Original problem-solving
In a Kazakhstani Classroom: Like teachers worldwide, you may have limited specialist support. The strategies in this toolkit are chosen for their practicality and low cost.

馃攧 Putting It All Together UDL

Important Note on Overlap: A pupil can be both autistic and dyslexic. The key is to focus on the individual's specific needs and strengths.

Your New Mindset:

  • See the student first, not the label
  • Look for strengths to build on, not just challenges to fix
  • Understand that behavior is communication
  • Focus on adapting your teaching, not changing the student
馃挕 Reflection Question: Think of one student in your class. What unique strength do they bring that you could leverage in your lessons next week?
